# Changelog

Append-only record of changes. Newest at the bottom.

---

## v0.1 — Session 1
Initial draft. Public information portal administered by the Electoral Commission;
standardised unbiased summaries based on verifiable facts; independent review for bias;
blanket ban on independent public promotion with removal from election as the penalty.

## v0.2 — Session 1
Structural revision following critique.
- Replaced "unbiased summary" with standardised *format* for unedited party statements;
  removed truth-adjudication in favour of procedural audit.
- Removed blanket ban on independent promotion; replaced with strengthened third-party
  regime (lower registration thresholds, spending caps, real-time disclosure, labelling).
- Added multi-party oversight board and post-election external review.
- Replaced "equal exposure" with "equitable access."
- Replaced disqualification-for-any-breach with proportional penalties; disqualification
  only by court order.

## v0.3 — Session 1
Removed all references to earlier versions from the proposal text so it stands alone.

## v0.4 — Session 1
Replaced the "Regulation of Independent Promotion" framing with a substantive **Money in
Politics** section.
- Added $30,000 donation cap per donor per cycle; ban on company and organisation
  donations.
- Added substantial reduction of the party and candidate spending ceiling.
- Added electoral vouchers (~$20 per voter per cycle).
- Retained real-time disclosure as a supporting measure, reframed.

## v0.5 — Session 1
Added **Justification and Evidence** section. Grounded the reforms in *Electoral
Commission v Watson* ("limit the influence of money," "equality of voice among
participants") and 2023 donation and third-party spending data.

## v0.6 — Session 1
Added **Protection of Electoral Deliberation** clause, built on the jury-duty analogy.
Confined to fraud and deception (fraudulent contrivance, impersonation of electoral
authority, misrepresentation of the electoral process, fabricated party communications).
Explicit exclusions for ordinary advocacy, opinion, satire, and a party's own claims.
Solicitor-General leave required for prosecution.

## v0.7 — Session 1
Added **Manipulative Electoral Advertising** clause targeting subliminal stimulation,
deceptive source attribution, dehumanising imagery, and manufactured authenticity, with a
s 5 explanatory note.

## v0.8 — Session 1
Replaced the universal pre-clearance model with the **tiered regime** now in s 8:
self-certification for ordinary advertising, pre-clearance only for prescribed
manipulative techniques, 48-hour deemed approval, rapid adjudication, correction as
default remedy, takedown on order and appealable, media and platform safe harbour,
individual-sharing exemption.

## v0.9 — 12 November 2026
Full consolidation and renumbering. **Clause numbers have changed** — map earlier
references as follows:

Also in v0.9: fixed a typographical duplication in the protected period definition;
removed the pinning of third-party spending caps to a specific election year; removed
non-New Zealand authority from the explanatory notes.

## v0.10 — 13 November 2026 (deliberation turn 1)
s 8.5: added a 24-hour duty to notify an applicant whether a pre-clearance application
is complete, a default that an application not rejected as incomplete in that period is
deemed complete, and a fixed start point ("of receiving it") for the 48-hour decision
clock. This closes the gap by which Commission silence could suspend the clock and defeat
the deemed-approval safeguard. No renumbering. Proposed by `electoral-administrator`,
approved 4-1; see `deliberation/turn-001-vote-summary.json`.

Follow-up recorded from the concurrences and dissent: harmonise the 48-hour start points
in s 8.5(3) and (5), provide for resubmission after an incompleteness notice, and ensure a
deemed-granted code does not confer platform immunity or bar a takedown order.
