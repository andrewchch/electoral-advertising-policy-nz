## 1. Donation cap and vouchers — both, or one?
The $30,000 cap constrains large money; vouchers inject public money. They pull in
slightly different directions. Commit to both as a package (more robust anti-plutocratic
position, larger fiscal and political undertaking), or prioritise one?

**Status:** open.

## 2. The spending ceiling figure
s 6.2 commits to "substantially lower" but no number. The current full-contest party limit
is in the millions; the ceiling needs to be low enough that a party funded by capped
donations can reach it, but high enough to allow a credible campaign. Needs modelling.

**Status:** open — requires costing work.

## 3. Reach/spend threshold for paid digital pre-clearance
s 8.12 refers to "a reach threshold prescribed by regulation." What is it? Too low and
small operators are captured; too high and the significant spenders escape.

**Status:** open.

## 4. Should the prescribed techniques list be longer?
Currently four: subliminal stimulation, deceptive source attribution through design,
synthetic media presented as authentic, manufactured grassroots endorsement. Candidates
for addition: exploitation of cognitive biases through numerical framing (the "9 new
taxes"
problem); micro-targeting on harvested personal data; dehumanising imagery.

**Consideration:** each addition widens the s 5 exposure. The "9 new taxes" case is the
hard one — adding a numerical-framing technique would capture it, but risks capturing
ordinary partisan emphasis.

**Status:** open — the central unresolved design question.

## 5. Does s 7 have enough teeth?
s 7 leaves the "9 new taxes" billboard lawful by design. Is that the right line, or should
there be a narrow offence of knowingly false statements of fact about a rival's policy
platform? Note the existing s 199A Electoral Act offence covers only the final days and
only deliberate falsehoods.

**Status:** open — linked to Q4.

## 6. Voucher cost and funding mechanism
~$20 × enrolled voters. Confirm the fiscal envelope and the split between redirected
existing public funding and new expenditure.

**Status:** open.

## 7. Who sits on the oversight board, and how are members appointed?
s 3 specifies the composition categories but not the appointment mechanism or tenure. Needs
a process that is independent of the government of the day.

**Status:** open.

## 8. Voting and amendment rules for the public consultation phase
Who may vote on amendments; what majority threshold; who applies approved changes to the
master document. See the separate platform discussion.

**Status:** open.
