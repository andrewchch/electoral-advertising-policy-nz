# Turn 003 — civil-libertarian

- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Branch:** pods/turn-003
- **Base:** 404d08c (v0.10, after turn 2 rejection)

## Issue identified

The completeness amendment in s 8.5 has three defects that the turn 1 concurrences and
dissent identified, and they are all prior-restraint problems.

First, there are two 48-hour clocks. Sub-clause 3 requires a decision within 48 hours of
*receiving* the application; sub-clause 5 deems the application granted if no decision is
made within 48 hours of *a complete application*. If an application is deemed complete at
hour 24, the outer restraint can read as 72 hours. A speaker cannot know which clock
governs.

Second, there is no resubmission rule. If the Commission rejects an application as
incomplete, the applicant must correct it, and the five-working-day lead time in
sub-clause 1 may expire in the meantime. Nothing says whether the corrected application
keeps its original date or restarts every period. The Commission can also issue a fresh
incompleteness notice on each resubmission, extending delay indefinitely without ever
making a reviewable decision.

Third, a code deemed granted under sub-clause 5 is issued without any substantive
decision, yet it is a "valid code" for s 8.6(2) and therefore gives platforms immunity,
and s 8.8(2) takedown is unavailable because the advertising has been "pre-cleared". A
thin or placeholder application filed late can therefore produce a deemed code for a
synthetic-media advertisement, with the strongest remedy unavailable while the lie
circulates.

These are not three separate policy questions. They are one question: what is the
decision period, and what follows from a deemed grant?

## Proposed change

Replace s 8.5(3), (5), and add sub-clauses 7 and 8:

> 3. The Commission must, within 24 hours of receiving an application, notify the applicant
>    whether it is complete and, if not, what is missing. An application not rejected as
>    incomplete within that period is deemed complete. The Commission must decide an
>    application within 48 hours of receiving it or, where the Commission has notified the
>    applicant that it is incomplete, within 48 hours of receiving the corrected application.
>
> 5. If no decision is made within the applicable 48-hour period under sub-clause 3, the
>    application is deemed granted and a code issued immediately.
>
> 7. An applicant may correct an application within 24 hours of an incompleteness notice. A
>    corrected application is taken to have been received on the date of the original
>    application for the purposes of sub-clause 1, but the 48-hour period runs from its
>    actual receipt. The Commission may reject an application as incomplete on one occasion
>    only, and the notice must be confined to the matters in sub-clause 2.
>
> 8. A code issued under sub-clause 5 is a deemed code. The Commission must record it on the
>    public register and review the advertising under s 8.7 within 3 working days. Section
>    8.8(2) applies to advertising published under a deemed code as if it had not been
>    pre-cleared.

Sub-clauses 1, 2, 4, and 6 are unchanged. No clause is renumbered.

## Reasoning

The amendment gives the scheme a single, knowable decision period. For a complete
application, the outer limit is 48 hours from receipt. For an application the Commission
has notified as incomplete, the applicant has 24 hours to correct and the Commission then
has 48 hours from the corrected application. The applicant controls the correction; the
Commission cannot extend the period by silence or by repeated deficiency notices. The
five-working-day lead time in sub-clause 1 is preserved by treating the corrected
application as received on the original date.

Sub-clause 8 deals with the one case where a code is issued without a substantive
decision. The code remains valid for publication — the deemed-approval rule is not
reversed — but it is recorded, reviewed, and does not shield the advertisement from the
takedown remedy that would have applied if no pre-clearance had been sought. That is the
minimum needed to stop a deemed code becoming a lawful channel for synthetic media.

The changes are speaker-protective. Section 14 NZBORA is engaged by pre-clearance; a
single clear clock and a bar on iterative incompleteness notices reduce the scope for
administrative delay. Section 8's s 5 justification depends on the deemed-approval rule
being real, not on a deemed code being immune from review.

## Risks and counter-arguments

*The "one occasion only" rule may force the Commission to refuse on the merits if it
misses a defect in the first notice.* That is the intended allocation of risk: the
Commission has 24 hours and the full application, and it retains the power to refuse under
sub-clause 4. The alternative — unlimited deficiency notices — lets the Commission delay
without a reviewable decision, which is the prior-restraint problem this turn exists to
fix.

*A deemed code that can be taken down is not really a deemed approval.* It is: the code
issues, the advertising can be published, and the platform immunity in s 8.6(2) still
applies. What sub-clause 8 removes is the immunity from the takedown remedy where a
prescribed technique is used and correction would not be adequate — the same test that
would apply if the promoter had never sought pre-clearance. The deemed-approval rule still
protects against a slow or captured Commission.

*The 3-working-day review adds workload.* Deemed codes should be rare; if they are common,
the Commission's intake is failing and the review is the least of the problems.
